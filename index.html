After the Eaton Fire: who acts, and when

During the fireJan 7–31, 2025
Jan 7, 6:18 pm
Fire starts

Wind-driven fire spreads into Altadena overnight; evacuation orders across the area.

Jan 2025
Claims open

The living-expense (ALE) clock starts on the date of loss. The state asks insurers for advance payments.

Jan 2025
Lead in the air

At one Los Angeles monitor, lead in fine particles rose about 110 times for several days (Baliaka et al., MMWR 2025).

Jan 2025
Damage inspection (DINS)

Teams walk every structure and grade it from Affected to Destroyed. Final count: 9,414 destroyed, 1,074 damaged.

Jan 31
Fire contained

14,021 acres burned; 19 people died.

First monthsFeb–May 2025
Feb 26
Hazardous waste removed (Phase 1)

EPA finishes removing batteries, chemicals and other hazards from burned lots within 30 days, then hands over to Phase 2.

Feb 2025
Caltech surface sampling

Cleaned surfaces inside and miles from the burn area: about 10% still at or above the federal lead limit.

May 2025
Smoke claims task force

The insurance commissioner forms a task force on smoke claims and cleanup.

May 22
Free soil lead testing

LA County Public Health tests yard soil downwind of the fire; about 26,000 homes invited.

First yearJun 2025–Jan 2026
Aug 29
Debris removal done (Phase 2)

The Army Corps of Engineers finishes clearing burned lots in Altadena, Pasadena and Sierra Madre.

Late 2025
Homes tested after cleanup

Residents’ group data: 63% of 50 professionally cleaned homes still had floor lead far above EPA screening levels; 36% of those tested had asbestos.

2025–
Long-term health study

LA Fire HEALTH Study follows 50 homes and their residents: air, dust, blood, hair and urine.

Jan 2026
One year on

Some standing homes are still sealed off; a windowsill can fail a lead test after four professional cleanings.

Year two2026–2027
Mar 13, 2026
Task force report

Finds survivors “falling through the gaps” in smoke testing and cleanup rules; calls for statewide standards.

Sep 15, 2026
AB 1795 signed

Smoke damage in the fire zone is presumed to come from the fire; insurers pay for testing and cannot end living expenses until the home is cleared.

Sep 15, 2026
AB 1642 signed

State cleanup standards: lead and asbestos by end of 2028, other contaminants by 2029. Heat damage inside walls is not covered.

Jan 7, 2027
24 months of living expenses

Minimum ALE period ends; up to 12 more months if rebuilding is delayed by things outside the owner’s control.
